# ADR-011: Adopt the Gurukul-era visual direction (reverses ADR-010's hero decision)

**Status**: Accepted — supersedes `ADR-010`'s visual conclusion
**Date**: This design phase (second revision)

## Context
`ADR-010` recorded a decision to keep the visual treatment abstract and typographic ("Direction A — The Guide"), rejecting the culturally-illustrated approach of a user-supplied reference on the grounds that the originating brief's §3 said to avoid "overly traditional/religious visual treatment."

After seeing the built result, the user judged it too cold and generically modern, and stated clearly that the reference image's aesthetic — warm, culturally grounded, evocative of the gurukul era — is what they want, explicitly noting they are *not* targeting the Hindu community specifically but want that ancient-Indian-cultural feeling.

This is a legitimate client decision made with full information: both directions were built and compared. The brief's §3 guardrail is therefore **amended by the brief's author**, not overridden unilaterally.

## Decision
Adopt a warm, Gurukul-era visual direction, specified in full in `docs/VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md`:

- **Palette**: warm ink ground (`#14100C`/`#1E1811`/`#2A2118`) replacing cool navy; parchment text (`#E6D9BE`) replacing pure white; brass gold retained (`#D4AF37`, SRS §13) but re-grounded so it reads as lamplight; terracotta/maroon/indigo as earth-pigment accents.
- **Typography**: Marcellus (display, inscriptional serif) + Cormorant Garamond (quotes) + Mukta (body, Ek Type — harmonises Devanagari with Latin), replacing Poppins/Inter.
- **Ornament**: temple/cusped arches, jali lattice texture, rosette dividers, manuscript corner brackets, diya lamp motif — all drawn as thin brass line-work in inline SVG.
- **Devanagari**: used as small decorative accents only; never load-bearing for comprehension.

## The boundary that is retained
Deity imagery, religious figures, and devotional symbols remain **out of scope** (`VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md` §2). The era is evoked through **architecture, material, pattern, light, and geometry** — not iconography. Rationale: the product serves anyone pursuing self-discipline regardless of faith; architecture reads as cultural heritage and is inviting, whereas deity imagery reads as religious affiliation, narrows the audience, and misrepresents what the product is. The user's own framing ("not specifically targeting the Hindu community") supports this boundary.

## Consequences
- `UX_ARCHITECTURE.md` §1 (design direction) and §4 (hero direction) are superseded on visual treatment; `docs/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`'s palette and typography sections are superseded by `VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md` §3–4.
- SRS §13's palette/type are now diverged from in two places (ground colour, text colour, font families) — recorded explicitly in `VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md` §3–4 rather than applied silently. The SRS brand gold is unchanged.
- **Nothing structural changes.** IA, journeys, screen inventory, dashboard composition (`ADR-010`'s layout patterns, which remain adopted), spacing scale, grid, breakpoints, motion principles, accessibility baseline, component inventory, and the entire system/API/database architecture are all unaffected. The reversal is visual only.
- All five built screens (Landing, Goal Selection, Onboarding, Recommendation, Dashboard) were rebuilt in this direction; the cool-navy versions are discarded.
- Ornament introduces a new review risk (gaudiness). Mitigated by the four ornament rules and the explicit failure-mode checklist in `VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md` §5 and §8.

## Confirmed by
User, directly, after comparing both built directions.
